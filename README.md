<h1> XFOIL Simulation - NACA 4-Series Aerofoil</h1>

<h2>Description</h2>
XFOIL is a free software developed by MIT that is used for the design and analysis of subsoinc aerofoils. Utilising the coordinates of a 2D aerofoil, Reynolds Number & Mach Number, XFOIL can calculate the pressure distribution on the airfoil and lift and drag characteristics. 

The effect of wing design on aircraft performance was explored. The main performance parameter that is being considered is minimum drag at cruise flight conditions. Two design variables were investigated, the Aspect ratio of the wing and the profile of the aerofoil. For simplification, the aerofoil follows the NACA 4-digit profile,. The effect of the performance of the wing design on the thrust required by the aircraft was also examined. The aircraft that was being considered is an autonomous micro air vehicle (MAV) that will be used in a future smart city. A typical mission for this MAV would include flying to the scene of an area of interest and carrying out an investigation of the location with video and images. The MAV is powered by an electric motor, meaning that the range and duration of the flight mission is limited by the low energy density of the batteries currently available. The MAV will need to have sufficient range and maneuverability to carry out its flight mission, this means that aerodynamic efficiency and control are vital to the success of this design.
